Converter categories

The converter changes a value from one unit to another within the same category. Choose a category, then select the starting and target units.

Length

Measures distance or size, from tiny nanometres to kilometres, miles and astronomical distances.

Weight / Mass

Compares how much matter an object contains. Includes metric units and common imperial units.

Volume

Measures how much space a liquid, gas or container occupies, such as millilitres, litres and gallons.

Speed

Measures distance travelled per unit of time, such as metres per second, kilometres per hour or miles per hour.

Temperature

Converts temperature scales such as Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin. Temperature conversions use offsets, not just multiplication.

Currency

Changes the numeric display between supported currencies using the converter's available rates. Rates may not be live.

Data Units

Measures digital information size, such as bits, bytes, kilobytes, megabytes, gigabytes and binary units.

Area

Measures a two-dimensional surface, such as square metres, hectares, acres or square feet.

Pressure

Measures force applied over an area, such as pascals, bar, atmospheres and psi.

Energy

Measures the ability to do work or transfer heat, such as joules, calories and kilowatt-hours.

Power

Measures how quickly energy is transferred or work is done, such as watts and horsepower.

Time

Converts durations, from milliseconds and seconds to minutes, hours, days and years.

Frequency

Measures how often something repeats each second, in hertz and larger multiples.

Force

Measures a push or pull, commonly in newtons, kilonewtons and pound-force.

Torque

Measures the turning effect of a force, commonly newton-metres and pound-feet.

Angle

Measures rotation or direction, usually in degrees or radians.

Density

Measures mass per unit of volume, useful for comparing how compact a material is.

Fuel Consumption

Compares how much fuel a vehicle uses over a distance, such as L/100 km or miles per gallon. Some units are inverse scales.

Flow

Measures how much fluid passes a point over time, for example litres per minute, cubic metres per second or gallons per minute.

Voltage

Measures electrical potential difference, the electrical 'push' that can drive current; measured in volts.

Current

Measures the rate of electric charge flow, in amperes (amps) and smaller units.

Resistance

Measures how strongly a material or component opposes electric current, in ohms.

Illuminance

Measures how much light reaches a surface, commonly in lux or foot-candles.

Data Rate

Measures how quickly digital data is transferred, such as bits per second, kilobits per second, megabits per second or bytes per second.

Acceleration

Measures the change in velocity per unit of time, such as metres per second squared or standard gravity.

Electric Charge

Measures the quantity of electricity, in coulombs, ampere-hours and smaller units.

Capacitance

Measures a component's ability to store electric charge per volt, in farads.

Inductance

Describes how strongly a circuit opposes changes in electric current, in henries.

Electrical Conductance

Measures how easily electric current flows, in siemens; it is the reciprocal of resistance.

Magnetic Flux

Measures the total magnetic field passing through a surface, in webers.

Magnetic Flux Density

Measures magnetic field strength per area, in teslas or gauss.

Angular Velocity

Measures how quickly an object rotates, in radians per second or revolutions per minute.

Dynamic Viscosity

Measures a fluid's resistance to flowing under shear, in pascal-seconds.

Kinematic Viscosity

Measures dynamic viscosity relative to fluid density, in square metres per second.

Thermal Conductivity

Measures how readily a material conducts heat, commonly in watts per metre-kelvin.

Specific Heat Capacity

Measures the energy needed to raise one kilogram of a substance by one kelvin.

Molar Concentration

Measures the amount of a substance per volume of solution, commonly in moles per litre.

Luminous Intensity

Measures light emitted in a particular direction, in candelas.

Amount of Substance

Measures the number of elementary entities, commonly in moles.

Quick glossary

HEX

A color notation using six hexadecimal digits for red, green and blue, for example #3B82F6. Each pair represents one color channel.

RGB

Red, Green and Blue. Each channel is usually a number from 0 to 255; mixing the three creates a screen color.

HSL

Hue, Saturation and Lightness. It describes a color by its color family, vividness and brightness.

Flow

Fluid flow rate: the volume of liquid or gas passing a point in a certain time. It is not the speed of an individual particle.

Bits vs Bytes

A bit is a single binary digit. A byte is normally 8 bits. Internet speeds are often shown in bits per second; file sizes are often shown in bytes.

Metric prefixes

kilo means 1,000; mega 1,000,000; giga 1,000,000,000. Binary prefixes such as kibi, mebi and gibi use powers of 1,024.
